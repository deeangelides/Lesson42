A wall nobody could climb

High in the Indian Himalayas, on a mountain called Meru, there is a wall of rock so difficult that for years people simply called it ‘unclimbable.’ It is known as the Shark’s Fin — a huge, smooth blade of granite that points straight up into the thin, freezing air, looking exactly like the fin of a giant shark slicing out of the mountainside. For more than thirty years, many of the world’s very best climbers had travelled from all over the globe to try to reach its top. One by one, every single one of them had failed, and the Shark’s Fin earned a fearsome reputation as one of the hardest climbs anywhere on Earth.

Why it was so hard

The Shark’s Fin is a steep, overhanging tower about 1,500 feet tall — taller than the tallest skyscrapers — and it sits more than 20,000 feet above the sea. What makes it so fierce is that it demands every kind of climbing at once. A climber must battle up walls of solid ice, haul themselves over bare rock, and inch up smooth stone with almost nothing to hold on to, all while carrying heavy bags of food and gear. Worst of all, the air so high up is so thin that every single step leaves you gasping for breath, and the biting cold can freeze your fingers in minutes. For decades, this deadly mix defeated even the greatest mountaineers in the world.

The team who took it on

In 2008, three American climbers decided to take on the challenge together: Conrad Anker, Jimmy Chin and Renan Ozturk. Conrad was the wise, experienced leader of the team; Jimmy Chin was not only a brilliant climber but also a world-famous photographer and filmmaker; and Renan was a gifted young climber and artist. Because Jimmy and Renan could film and take photographs as they climbed, the team was able to capture their whole adventure on camera. They set off full of hope. For days they fought their way upward through howling, freezing storms, sleeping in a tiny tent that hung from the sheer side of the cliff, thousands of feet above the ground below.

Then disaster struck

A fierce storm trapped them on the wall for days, and their food slowly began to run out. Painfully, step by step, they pushed on — until they were just 100 metres from the summit. They were so close they could almost reach out and touch it. But they were exhausted, starving and running out of time, and to carry on would have been far too dangerous. Heartbroken, the three friends made the hardest decision of all: they turned back. Their dream was over… for now.

Never giving up

For three long years, the friends could not forget the Shark’s Fin. It haunted their dreams. So in 2011, they went back to the mountain to try all over again. Once more they battled the cold, the thin air, the brutal climbing and their own aching exhaustion — but this time, they refused to be beaten. Metre by metre, they fought their way up the great granite blade… and at last, they pulled through and stood together on the very top. They had done what everyone had said was impossible, becoming the first people ever to climb the Shark’s Fin.

Sharing the adventure

Jimmy Chin later turned all the footage they had filmed into an award-winning documentary film, so that people everywhere could share in their extraordinary adventure. The story of the Shark’s Fin is now famous around the world — a reminder that with courage, friendship, and the refusal to give up, even the ‘impossible’ can be achieved.

🤔 Section 4 · What is a phrasal verb?

Verb + little word = new meaning!

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word (like up, on, out, back or through) that together make a new meaning.

give+up=quit! 🏳️

“Give up” doesn’t mean to give anything away — the little word changes the whole meaning!

4Meet Some More!4 phrasal verbs
🐶look after

= take care of
“I look after my puppy.”

🔍find out

= discover
“Let’s find out the answer!”

🎒set off

= begin a journey
“We set off at dawn.”

🏃carry on

= continue
“Carry on reading, please.”

🔤 Section 5 · Articles

A, An & The

These tiny words are called articles. They come before a noun — but sometimes we leave them out altogether! 🕵️

5A or An? Listen to the sound!any one, not a special one
a

before a consonant sound 🗣️

a mountain 🏔️a rope 🪢a tent ⛺
an

before a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u) 🗣️

an avalanche ❄️an ice axe ⛏️an egg 🥚
🕵️ Sneaky one!

We say an hour ⏰ — because the h is silent, so the word sounds like it starts with a vowel: “ow-er”!

5The, and When to Leave It Out3 rules
🎯 the

one special thing

we both know which one.

“They climbed the Shark’s Fin.”

👀 a ➜ the

first time, then after

a the first time, the once we know it.

“I saw a climber. The climber waved.”

🚫 no article

leave it out!

before most names, and plurals or snow-type nouns in general.

“Jimmy flew to India. Snow is cold.”
